1.1 Exercise 6.1

Denote \(\bar{R}_{(j)}^2\) as the adjusted R-squared of estimation in column \(j \in \{1,2,3\}\). Note that

\[ 1-R^2 = \frac{SSR}{TSS} \]

we can compute:

\[ \bar{R}^2_{(1)} = 1 - \frac{n - 1}{n - k - 1} \frac{SSR}{TSS} = 1 - \frac{7178 - 1}{7178 - 2 - 1} (1 - 0.165) \approx 0.1648 \]

\[ \bar{R}^2_{(2)} = 1 - \frac{n - 1}{n - k - 1} \frac{SSR}{TSS} = 1 - \frac{7178 - 1}{7178 - 3 - 1} (1 - 0.182) \approx 0.1817 \]

\[ \bar{R}^2_{(3)} = 1 - \frac{n - 1}{n - k - 1} \frac{SSR}{TSS} = 1 - \frac{7178 - 1}{7178 - 6 - 1} (1 - 0.185) \approx 0.1843 \]

1.5 Exercise 7.2

  1. Prepare:

\[ \left\{\begin{aligned} &H_0: \beta_1 = 0 \\ &H_1: \beta_1 \neq 0 \end{aligned}\right. \]

Let the significance level be 5%.

Calculate: The 95% confidence interval for \(\beta_1\) is

\[ \hat{\beta}_1 \pm Z_{0.025} \cdot SE(\hat{\beta}_1) = 0.352 \pm 1.96 \times 0.021 = [0.3108, 0.3932] \]

Conclusion:

Since 0 is not in the 95% confidence interval, we reject \(H_0\). There is significant evidence that high school earnings differences exist.

  1. Prepare:

\[ \left\{\begin{aligned} &H_0: \beta_2 = 0 \\ &H_1: \beta_2 \neq 0 \end{aligned}\right. \]

Let the significance level be 5%.

Calculate: The 95% confidence interval for \(\beta_2\) is

\[ \hat{\beta}_2 \pm Z_{0.025} \cdot SE(\hat{\beta}_2) = 0.458 \pm 1.96 \times 0.021 = [0.4168, 0.4992] \]

Since 0 is not in the 95% confidence interval, we reject \(H_0\). There is significant evidence that male-female earnings differences exist.

1.7 Exercise 7.4

  1. To perform an appropriate hypothesis test on regional differences, we conduct the following procedures step-by-step:
  1. Claim the null hypothesis:

\[ \left\{\begin{aligned} &H_0: \beta_4 = \beta_5 = \beta_7 = 0 \\ &H_1: \text{Not all are zero} \end{aligned}\right. \]

In other words, we are testing if the difference between restricted model and unrestricted model is statistically significant.

  • Restricted Model (\(\beta_4 = \beta_5 = \beta_7 = 0\))

\[ \text{AWE} = \beta_0 + \sum_{i=1}^3 \beta_i X_i + u \]

  • Unrestricted Model

\[ \text{AWE} = \beta_0 + \sum_{i=1}^3 \beta_i X_i + \beta_4 X_4 + \beta_5 X_5 + \beta_6 X_6 + v \]

  1. Next, we derive the pivotal quantity:

\[ \begin{aligned} \phi &= \frac{(SSE_r - SSE_u) / q}{(SSE_u) / (n - k_u - 1)} \sim F(q, n - k_u - 1) \end{aligned} \]

where \(SSE_r\) is the SSE of the restricted model, and \(SSE_u\) is the SSE of the unrestricted model. Besides, \(n\) is the number of samples, \(k_u\) is the number of independent variables used in the unrestricted model, and \(q\) is the number of restricted variables.

In this case, \(n=10,000\), \(k_u=6\) and \(q=3\), therefore,

\[ \phi \sim F(3, 10000-6-1) \sim F(3, 9993) \]

  1. Given \(\alpha = 0.05\), we can construct the rejection region (RR):

\[ RR = \{\phi \mid \phi > F_{0.05}(3, 9993) = 2.6058\} \]

  1. Finally, since

\[ \phi^* = 21.87 \in RR \]

we reject \(H_0\). There is significant evidence that regional differences exist.

    1. Consider the model in column (3). The expected difference between Juan and Mel is \(\hat{\beta}_4\), hence the 95% confidence interval for the expected difference between Juan and Mel is:

\[ \hat{\beta}_4 \pm Z_{0.025} \cdot SE(\hat{\beta}_4) = 0.175 \pm 1.96 \times 0.037 = [0.1025, 0.2475] \]

  1. The expected difference between Juan and Ari is:

\[ \beta_4 \times (X_{4,\text{Juan}} - X_{4,\text{Ari}}) + \beta_7 \times (X_{7,\text{Juan}} - X_{7,\text{Ari}}) = \beta_4 - \beta_7 \]

A 95% confidence interval could be constructed using the general methods. In this case, an easy way to do this is to omit East from the regression and replace it with \(X_6 = \textit{West}\). In this new regression, the coefficient on North measures the difference in wages between the North and East, and a 95% confidence interval can be computed directly.

      From the preceding estimation result, we have both \(\textit{AA}\) and \(\textit{BA}\) not individually significant under \(5\%\) significance level because both \(p\)-values are greater than 0.05.

    2. Joint significance

      linearHypothesis(
  reg_2c,
  c("AA = 0", "BA = 0"),
  test = "F"
)

      Since \(p\)-value is greater than \(0.05\), we can’t reject the null hypothesis that both coefficients are equal to \(0\). There is no evidence that the effects of college degree exist.

    3. Change in \(R^2\)

      By comparing the estimation results in question (a), (c), we have the
  adjusted $R^2$ from specification (1) is greater than (2). In other
  words, adding these two variables doesn't improve explaining the
  variation in wage.

  In conclusion, we suggest that the two dummy variables $AA$ and $BA$
  should not be kept in the regression specification.
